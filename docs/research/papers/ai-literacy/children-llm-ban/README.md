# The Case for an Immediate Ban on Children's LLM Chatbot Use

**Source:** AI and Ethics (Springer), 2026
**Authors:** Scott Robbins, Inga Blundell
**DOI:** 10.1007/s43681-026-01014-5
**Link:** https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/en/?backlinks_to=10.1007/s11023-025-09757-6

## Key Findings

- Draws parallels between delayed regulatory response to social media harm and the current LLM adoption among children
- Advocates precautionary ban on children's LLM use based on four conditions: epistemic uncertainty, credible scientific indication of serious harm, irreversibility of damage, and proportionality of response
- Early empirical findings suggest LLMs can contribute to: emotional dependency, impaired problem-solving skills, exacerbated mental-health vulnerabilities among children
- Shifts burden of proof to proponents of children's LLM access

## Implications for CoEvo

- Supports the thesis that passive AI consumption (asking AI for answers without active engagement) impairs problem-solving skills
- Strong evidence for the "cognitive dependency" risk that CoEvo's objective verification model addresses
- Precautionary principle aligns with CoEvo's approach to AI-assisted learning: structured, verified, and metacognitively aware
