# Potential Risks of Generative AI Integration into K-12 Education: A Scoping Review

**Source:** Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence, 2026
**Authors:** Sisi Tao, Min Lan, Minjuan Wang, Hui Li (Education University of Hong Kong)
**DOI:** 10.1016/J.CAEAI.2026.100561
**Link:** https://scholar.cnki.net/Detail/index/GARJ2021_6/SJES2D0D3BDB75906074B03D1F6A3F541F4E

## Key Findings

- Synthesizes 22 empirical studies on GenAI risks in K-12 contexts
- Three risk domains identified:
  - **Psychological wellbeing**: emotional disconnection, social isolation
  - **Intellectual agency**: cognitive dependency, distorted self-assessment, erosion of creative authorship
  - **Ecological**: limited institutional readiness, unclear governance, equity gaps, privacy concerns
- Promising mitigation strategies: process-over-product task design, using GenAI for scaffolding (hints) not direct solutions, embedding tools within critical AI literacy curricula
- Concludes: without developmentally responsive governance, GenAI risks displacing "productive struggle" and authentic expression necessary for learning

## Implications for CoEvo

- Directly supports CoEvo's approach: objective verification (not self-assessment), structured review, preventing cognitive dependency
- "Distorted self-assessment" category maps to CoEvo's core value proposition — people can't judge their own learning accuracy
- Scaffolding over solutions aligns with CoEvo's "verify don't supply answers" design philosophy
